# Sound direction and playback

## A room to linger in

The score should support reading and browsing. It uses one composed harmonic
phrase, a sparse original melody, soft electric keys and an understated pulse.
Eight related arrangements suggest the changing vocabulary of electronic
sound between the 1950s and the 2020s. They do not reproduce console startup
sounds, game music, commercial recordings, or recognizable hardware jingles.

The arrangement is generated by `music/compose.py`, with every chord and
melodic note visible in source. Only low-level percussion texture uses a
deterministic random source. The original score carries the project license;
it does not need a streaming service, network access, or a third-party account.
Museum videos and other historical media retain their own online provenance.

## Musical continuity

Every arrangement lasts exactly 64 seconds: sixteen four-beat bars at 60 BPM.
The shared progression passes through D minor, F major, B-flat major and an
A suspended harmony. Common notes connect the voicings. Circular note tails
and circular early reflections preserve the natural release across a loop.
Periodic spectral filtering keeps the sound soft without introducing a filter
state discontinuity at the boundary.

All eight era files run against one continuously advancing clock. A year
selects its containing decade: 1989 selects the 1980s arrangement, rather than
a blend dominated by the 1990s. A change starts on the next audio callback and
completes a constant-sum crossfade in exactly 9,600 output frames, or 200 ms.
Retargeting during a fade starts from the current gains. Nothing restarts or
seeks, including arrangements whose current gain is zero. This constant-sum
blend is deliberate: the arrangements share material, so an equal-power blend
could make the middle of a transition louder.

The revised orchestration makes decade changes more perceptible: close sine
counterlines give way to drawbar-like chords, syncopated analog bass, glassy FM
arpeggios, downtempo mallets, digital plucks, wide breathing pads and a hybrid
sub pulse. The harmony and phrase stay aligned. Matching the arrangements'
RMS before shared mastering keeps a change from merely becoming louder.

## Native integration contract

`museum::Music` is in `src/museum/music.hpp` and `.cpp`.

1. Construct the object, then call `init(directory)` with the installed
   directory containing `1950.ogg` through `2020.ogg` before starting audio.
2. If initialization fails, record `error()` and continue starting the app.
   `render()` always fills silence when unavailable. Partial sets are discarded.
3. UI code calls `select_year(float)`, `volume(0..1)`, `muted(bool)`, and
   `video_active(bool)`. Setters use atomics and do not touch decoder state.
4. The one platform audio callback calls `render(float*, frames)` at 48 kHz
   stereo. This overwrites its buffer. Add video PCM and UI sounds afterward,
   then use the platform limiter and output conversion.
5. Keep the object alive until the output thread stops. Do not reinitialize
   or destroy it while a callback is rendering.

No decoding, allocation, mutex, file operation, or platform API occurs inside
`render()`. Vorbis is decoded to PCM16 once during initialization. The eight
loops occupy 49,152,000 bytes of PCM. The platform wrapper owns the actual
audio output, so music and video never open competing audio devices.

The default volume is 0.42, with a gentle initial entrance. Volume, mute and
video ducking use sample-time smoothing. Video ducking lowers the score by
30 dB. Muting does not stop its clock, so returning to the museum preserves
the musical phrase. Persistent UI settings are handled by the application,
which should pass the stored music volume/mute back to this object.

## Verification

`music/measurements.json` describes the uncompressed masters.
`music/encoded-measurements.json` checks the files after Vorbis compression.
`music/verify.py` verifies exact frame counts, finite samples, useful headroom,
loop boundaries, adjacent-era blend peaks, listening-level matching and
measurable arrangement differences. Current encoded arrangements differ by at
least 55% of one arrangement's RMS between adjacent decades; their maximum
adjacent correlation is 0.847. These are signal measurements, not a substitute
for listening.

The host test `music/test_music.cpp` compiles against the same native renderer
and stb_vorbis implementation as the console build. It covers missing files,
the decoded memory budget, block-size invariant playback, more than two loop
boundaries, exact 200 ms transitions, immediate fade start, rapid retargeting,
constant-sum gains and the shared playhead through mute and video ducking. At
default volume its tested peak was 0.15598 and the loop-boundary step was
0.00225. Encoded peak stays below 0.398; the eight Ogg files total 2,302,215
bytes while decoded memory remains 49,152,000 bytes.

A host stress test repeatedly retargeting all eight eras used 0.114 seconds of
CPU time to render 213.33 seconds of stereo audio on the development computer.
This is a bounded host measurement, not a PS5 CPU or frame-rate claim. The
listenable preview `music/previews/Museum - A Walk Through Time.ogg` changes
arrangement every eight seconds with the same 200 ms crossfade.

These are host checks of the composition and native renderer. The earlier
score was heard during the user's PS5 tests; the revised orchestration and
shorter transition still need their own console listening check. Offline
analysis does not establish the quality of that new console output.

### Startup timing investigation (20 October 2026)

Five consecutive fresh `Music` instances, built with the production loader
and stb_vorbis decoder, loaded all eight installed Ogg files in 216.769,
216.148, 214.349, 212.697 and 215.749 milliseconds on the development host
(mean 215.142 ms). This includes reading the 2,302,215 compressed bytes,
allocating the output and decoding all 49,152,000 bytes of PCM. Filesystem
caches were not cleared, so these are not controlled cold-storage timings.
The evidence is `build/music-startup-benchmark.log`.

This does not measure PS5 startup. The console's startup phase diagnostics
now bracket interface and music initialization so that an actual console
measurement can distinguish them. The captured slow-session log instead
shows recurring roughly 517 ms display draws and expensive object resolves;
music performs no further decoding after initialization. Consequently no
49 MB bundled-PCM expansion, extra worker or mutable cache was introduced
for this investigation. Shared phase and the 200 ms transition are unchanged.

### Navigation sound palette

`music/compose_navigation.py` creates 25 short, original 48 kHz stereo PCM16
clips in `assets/audio/museum-navigation/glass`, covering 18 cue types. They
use D, F, A and C, with a soft felt/FM attack and quiet early reflections.
Small ascending and descending gestures distinguish confirmation, returning
and opening a panel. No commercial game audio or external recordings enter
these files. Their total WAV size is 1,173,256 bytes; decoded storage is
2,344,312 bytes. Existing paper and glass effects remain untouched.

The new SoundBank root has its own mandatory `glass/index.txt`, because the
PS5 `/app0` sandbox does not provide reliable directory enumeration. The
optional `SoundBank::set_pitch_jitter(0.f)` retains authored tuning; other
users of the toolkit keep their existing +/-3% default. Loading and WAV
decoding happen before audio starts. Playback posts the existing fixed mixer
commands and performs no new allocations, I/O or synchronization in its
audio callback.

`music/test_navigation.cpp` exercises the actual SoundBank and mixer. It
checks 25 accepted files, all 18 cue names, exact PCM pitch with jitter
disabled, and bit-identical old default pitch behavior. A 1,200-action run
at ten actions per second produced no dropped commands, released all voices,
and reached peak 0.10742. The host bank load took 158.169 ms with uncontrolled
filesystem caches; this is not a PS5 startup measurement.

The 26-second preview `music/previews/Museum - Navigation Sound Palette.ogg`
contains a dry cue sequence followed by the same cues over the score, rendered
through the actual mixer at UI-bus gain 0.18 and music gain 0.48. Restart's
source clip compensates for its upstream SFX-bus routing without changing
the study effects bus. The mixed PCM peak was 0.18002, with no clipping.
The score continues its phase through a 1980-to-2020 crossfade. Console
audibility and the user's preferred balance still require listening there.
