## Editorial promise

Follow people making consequential choices. Open with a concrete situation: a composer revising a melody to fit a jump, an engineer trying to fit a cartridge into a product, or a player keeping a world alive after its official story ends. Explain what changed, whose work made it possible and what evidence survives. Use plain English. Avoid the language of inevitable progress, a parade of masterpieces or a single chain of great men inventing everything.

The existing game entries form the chronological backbone. The new feature layer connects them through design, art, music, engineering, labour, commerce, communities and preservation. The register gives each feature a distinct question and a proposed research window. A range is not a claimed event date. Specific dates only enter the timeline once a source identifies what actually happened: demonstration, publication, regional release, announcement, incorporation or later commemoration.

|2000s |Who produced the new connected worlds?|MMOcommunity labour; digital storefronts and ownership expectations; DS/Wii interaction; mobile before/after app stores; Pune as a documented production case; adaptive audio.|
|2010s |Which new voices, tools and audiences changed the medium?|Small tools and personal stories; accessibility work led by players; regional independent scenes; streaming; labour/credit; visual and musical authorship.|

There is no per-decade quota. The early decades need a few well-evidenced origins, not invented events to fill space. A current decade remains open: distinguish a reported launch from a forecast, and dated evidence from an assessment of lasting influence.

## Named creators and teams

The backlog makes room for programming, design and production as different jobs. Proposed creator profiles include Miyamoto and collaborators, Sakaguchi, Kojima, Mikami, Sid Meier, Will Wright, Carmack, Romero, Rebecca Heineman, Brenda Romero, Amy Hennig, Jane Jensen, Nasir Gebelli, Keita Takahashi, Kim Swift, Yokoi and Iwata. These are research commissions; a familiar name does not count as a completed biography. Expand existing exhibits or attach a connected feature rather than creating another birth-date node.

Use original game credits, first-person interviews and production documents together. An interview records what a participant remembers; it does not automatically settle disputed chronology or the contributions of absent colleagues. Credit porting, localization, QA, sound engineering, animation and outsourced production when the evidence names them. Do not turn a successful founder into the sole maker of an entire studio's work.

The people layer also explicitly includes Addis, Bailey, Shaw, Bunten, Tramis, Weisbecker, Lawson and Smith. [The Strong's Video Soft research](https://www.museumofplay.org/blog/jerry-lawson-video-soft-and-the-history-of-the-first-black-owned-video-game-development-company/) and [Ed Smith's first-person interview](https://www.vintagecomputing.com/index.php/archives/1230/ed-smith-interview) offer concrete starting points beyond repeating Channel F's launch. [The Sumerian Game finding aid](https://www.museumofplay.org/app/uploads/2021/09/Finding-Aid-to-the-Sumerian-Game-collection_2021.pdf) identifies surviving classroom material; its archival dates must not be substituted for a retail-release date.

## Art and music have their own histories

Art is not limited to cover pictures. Research readability, sprite animation, loading screens, illustration adapted for low-resolution machines, polygon environments, camera framing, typography and material interfaces. Profiles for Kazuko Shibuya, Yoshitaka Amano and Yoji Shinkawa must distinguish concept images from the people who realized them in the game. Museum displays should identify actual edition, region, platform and original/remaster state.

Music deserves a connected path rather than an anonymous playlist. Proposed subjects include Kondo, Tanaka, Hubbard, Koshiro, Shimomura, Uematsu, Mitsuda, Wise, Yamaoka, Curry, Wintory, Korb/Barrett, Raine and Phillips, with source work still to do. [Kondo's Nintendo interview](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/wii/super-mario-all-stars/0/3/) gives a specific design question: music was revised around Mario's movement. The [Library of Congress interview](https://lcweb2.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/koji-kondo-interview.pdf) provides another first-person source. Do not infer an entire chip's capabilities from a composer's shorthand description of one production.

Composition, arrangement, performance, sound design and driver programming need separate credits. Document actual hardware sound and adaptive transitions. A video being publicly viewable does not establish permission to redistribute its audio; use authorized publisher/composer recordings and retain provenance. No invented equalizer bars should imply the actual score is playing when it is not.

[Cyan's collection](https://library.gamehistory.org/repositories/2/resources/228) offers production evidence for image-making and performance. Its [Riven ADR session record](https://library.gamehistory.org/repositories/2/digital_objects/909) is a specific sound-production anchor, not an interchangeable documentary thumbnail. Collection permissions must be read at item level.

## A global history needs local evidence

The starting catalogue and familiar English-language sources favour Japan, North America and Western Europe. The backlog commissions distinct research for Brazil, the Caribbean, South Korea, China, India, Eastern Europe, Africa, Southwest Asia/NorthAfrica and Indigenous creators. Country labels here identify a research need, not proof that one example represents a region.

Tectoy should connect local distribution, translation and audiences in Brazil. [Muriel Tramis's own interview](https://news.xbox.com/fr-fr/2021/03/10/les-portraits-du-jeu-video-muriel-tramis/) gives a Caribbean/French creative account which cannot be replaced by a generic national-industry paragraph. [Ubisoft's 2016 studio profiles](https://static9.cdn.ubisoft.com/ubigroupstatic/en-US/global/img/company/Studio_profiles_September_2016_English_268341.pdf) document Pune's work; they are one corporate perspective, not the history of all Indian games.

Research Chinese and Korean PC/online cultures with local-language contemporary sources and named participants. Research Polish, Czech, Ukrainian and Russian production separately. For African and Southwest Asian coverage, find named creators, particular studios and surviving works before assigning an anchor date; do not manufacture a continental first. Distinguish work made in a place, work by diasporic creators and work depicting that place.

[ACMI'sUmurangi Generation record](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/122801--umurangi-generation/) supports a particular artist-led context, while [Jarra Karalinar Steel's commission](https://www.acmi.net.au/works/121855--more-than-just-a-game/) is another specific form. Māori histories and Australian First Nations histories are not interchangeable. Use creators' preferred names/nations, and ask about permission before reproducing culturally restricted material.

## Business, communities and contested histories

The business layer should explain retail, licensing, distribution, publishers, engines, digital storefronts and production schedules through concrete cases. US 1983 retail contraction must not become a worldwide end of gaming. Distinguish a console announcement, a Japanese launch, a North American launch and a later European edition. Hardware clones, imports and local adaptations require exact identification, not an assumed counterfeit label.

Communities include operators, magazine readers, BBS users, modders, tournament organizers, MMO administrators, translators, repairers and disabled players contributing design knowledge. Emulation is a valid and important preservation and access practice; explain the particular implementation and rights of the assets rather than disparaging the activity. Do not distribute game assets on the assumption that software source is open.

Research gender, sexuality, racial exclusion, censorship/ratings, violence controversies, monetization and working conditions where evidence connects them to the subject. Treat allegations as attributed claims with a date and response; distinguish a court finding from an unresolved dispute. Do not turn individual testimony into a universal experience or reduce a creator to discrimination. Make room for joy, craft and ordinary player knowledge alongside conflict.

## Preservation is a practice, not a download button

Connect the [Video Game History Foundation](https://library.gamehistory.org/), [ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/about/videogames-museum/), [The Strong](https://www.museumofplay.org/collections/icheg/) and [Computerspielemuseum Berlin](https://www.computerspielemuseum.de/Museum/27-Ueber-das-Museum.htm) through their actual collections and methods. No shared open API or universal image licence is implied. ACMI CC0 metadata does not licence every associated photograph or film. An original object, replica, restoration and digital reconstruction need visibly different captions.

The VGHF/SPN availability study concerns a defined UScommercial sample. Its often-quoted 87 percent must not become 87 percent of all games worldwide being lost. Retained copies, purchasability, authorized research access and continuing server operation are different questions. Server-dependent games also involve social practices, service code and testimony which an isolated client file does not preserve.

## Production sequence and completion gates

1. Preserve the whole existing Playhub article and source-language text with stable runtime links. Resolve remaining date semantics and duplicate destinations before adding more timeline nodes.
2. Strengthen the most important existing people, art and music stops using the named feature register. Work in small source-complete batches; do not replace missing evidence with generic prose.
3. Commission region-specific research in parallel, beginning with a real person/work/document and an identified local source. A collection homepage is a discovery lead, not a verified date or claim.
4. Select photographs, films, recordings and models for each article. Every selected asset needs identity, creator, rights, source page, edition/date context and sufficient genuine resolution. A model is optional, and only hardware topics request one by default.
5. Write plain English around people, stakes and choices. Each paragraph must have traceable evidence. Preserve quotations as quotations and licensed adaptations as adaptations.
6. Localize full sections while preserving paragraph indices, sources, captions and date semantics. Distinguish untranslated fallback from a finished localized article.
7. Review on the actual PS5: legible text, readable art, usable sources, media failure states, rotation/input timing and memory. A desktop GL pass is not a physical-console performance result.

A feature is complete only when researched, written, fact-checked, illustrated or explicitly documented as text-led, credited, localized as promised, and inspected in the native layout. The 140 JSON entries are plans at different research stages. None includes a fabricated article body or pretends that a broad archive link verifies all claims.

## Register contract

Each entry includes a collision-free feature ID, proposed title, research-window precision/semantics, story angle, region/theme, related existing IDs, explicit source-verification level, remaining evidence, per-media need/status and a delivery gate. Source statuses distinguish a verified institutional object or first-person interview from an archive discovery lead. Suggested cross-links require editorial review; they are not a semantic-deduplication proof.

This plan complements the root's complete article register. The existing corpus, feature commissions and unresolved regional research remain separate so a progress total can never silently count a planned article as one the visitor can already read.
