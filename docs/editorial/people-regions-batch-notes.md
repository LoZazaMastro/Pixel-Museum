## Evidence decisions

**Dona Bailey.** The Arcade Attack participant interview supplies the successive production tasks, focus group, arcade testing and May 1981 shipping recollection. Leigh Alexander's 27 August 2007 Gamasutra interview supplies Bailey's explanation of programming through language and her later teaching/tool-access concerns. The old article introduction's 1980 shorthand is not adopted as the game's release year. Exact 1981 shipping detail remains attributed retrospective testimony, with no invented launch day. Her view that she hoped everyone would enjoy the game is preserved without substituting a gender stereotype.

**Dani Bunten Berry.** Arthur Leyenberger's January 1985 Antic interview is contemporary evidence for publishing support, the earlier real-time stock-market idea and Seven Cities disk-only tests. The original 1983 M.U.L.E. manual supplies the exact mechanical examples: resource consequences and Tournament-only private product auctions. The manual's historical name is kept in its bibliography while museum voice uses Dani Bunten Berry and she/her. No quotations or essentialist claims about her identity are added. The Seven Cities example is explicitly another game, not an incorrectly attributed M.U.L.E. feature.

**Muriel Tramis.** The 10 March 2021 Xbox France interview and the CNC's specific interview/profile are directly paraphrased from French. The CNC source identifies Philippe Truca, Patrick Chamoiseau, the 1987 release and the 1902 setting. The work is treated as an authored historical representation, not an exhaustive account of slavery or Caribbean history. Firstness language from source introductions is not repeated. Tramis's explanation of intent is distinguished from evidence of how every player responded.

**Tectoy.** The company's specific history/timeline supports the 1989 console anchor. Stefano Arnhold's 16 November 2015 interview supports manufacturing, local distribution and the Mônica licensing negotiation; Alexandre Pagano's 31 July 2016 interview supplies drawing/tool workflow and his player-support career. Arnhold and Pagano have different organisational perspectives. Pagano's assertion that Master System had already been discontinued globally at the Brazilian launch is not used, nor are his uncertain rough totals treated as a production census. No unsupported extra technical detail about reverse-engineering or text encoding is added.

**Rebecca Heineman.** The creator's Doom 3DO archive README is the primary account of her starting material, schedule and conversion decisions. Lewis White's 2 May 2025 VideoGamer interview supplies her archive history and explicit limits: source survives without all early notes/work-in-progress assets, and possession does not automatically permit public release. The narrative does not inherit another outlet's framing of an absolutely complete recovered archive. No copyrighted game data was copied for this editorial task.

**Jarra Karalinar Steel.** ACMI's original How I See It announcement and the artist's complete 17 February 2023 film transcript are the actual evidence. The work-record page repeatedly timed out and is not cited as though unseen details had been read. The transcript identifies Charlotte Allingham, Peter Adams, Dolly, specific city names, Blackwood Wattle and the cabinet/floor motifs. The artist's cultural self-identification is preserved, and Country is not replaced by a generic geopolitical label. The official transcript links the actual film, YouTube `yUclszHB0vQ`; availability and embedding remain a separate media review. No motif was extracted to decorate the app.

**Gunpei Yokoi.** The Game & Watch group interview provides countable technical evidence: seventy-two display segments, twenty-eight used for digits and forty-four for the play image. Kano explicitly says he did not witness the train-story origin. The November 2016 Nintendo interview with Sakamoto is a separate publication and supplies the floating/combat request, distinct arcade/home contributions and the bubble experiment. The commonly repeated directional-pad patent attribution was deliberately excluded: the inspected US4687200 record names Ichiro Shirai. A different Yokoi/Okada multi-screen patent was inspected but is not needed for these four cards and is not used to manufacture a firstness claim.

**Satoru Iwata.** The Wii Remote and Wii Fit interviews are two distinct conversations/publications. The narrative follows exact exchanges, including Takeda's small interface teams, Ashida's changing play habits, two different routes to balance measurement and the practical width/cost disagreement. These are edited company-produced primary accounts, not neutral independent inquiries. Neither conversation is used as medical evidence, and the Wii-era 2006 anchor is not a claim that the later Wii Fit interview occurred that year.
