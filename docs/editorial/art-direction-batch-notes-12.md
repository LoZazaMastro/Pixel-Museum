- Mario’s moustache/overalls account was read but not reused: the existing Super Mario Bros. source article already covers it. The sprite study instead adds the named Shibuya/Ishii/Tokita workflow and distinguishes original-game CRT mountains from FFIII job design.
- Shibuya’s 2013 interview and Ishii’s 2020 recollection are separate testimony. Their accounts do not establish one universal Amano-to-sprite pipeline. The Square page explicitly says its current republication preserves a 2020 interview.
- The Sonic CD collection is one conservative publication budget even though it contains several original interviews. The separate 2013 Hoshino interview is credited to Diogo Miguel. This case does not overwrite Naka/Ohshima/Yasuhara’s original Sonic history.
- The Amano 1990 excerpt’s original publication is not identified by its translator. That bibliographic limitation is retained, not repaired by inventing a magazine. His 2024 Uniqlo interview is separately dated. Independent review may require a stronger original locator before admission.
- Konami’s Shinkawa companion-artbook page is undated; the filename used for research is not evidence of a 2001 interview date. MGS2’s release anchors the work, while the explicitly dated 2019 account concerns Left Alive and its named producer/director.

It is not a worldwide count or an assertion that every user could play Wii Sports. Local urllib returned403; the exact notice was fully read through the web tool.
- Rampancy’s migrated Griesemer header reads2001, but the page explicitly says originally posted10May2000. Only design intentions are assigned to that early interview; original Xbox controls/HUD are checked against the2001 manual, not Anniversary.
- The Sega.com Naka interview survives at The Sonic Stadium with Marc Cellucci and translator Osamu Shibamiya credited. It is not the inaccessible GameSpot interview. PSO card rules are checked against the original Dreamcast manual; no current official server operation is implied.
